Website Mission

Our mission is to present vaccination coverage, reported disease cases and infection rates in a clear and accessible way. The website helps users compare data across countries, years and economic groups, including changes in population-based vaccination rates and infection rates above the global benchmark. We show the country, year and unit clearly and explain unfamiliar terms using simple, informative and unbiased language.

How to use this website

  1. Choose your question. Use coverage for country and region comparisons, or infections for economic groups.
  2. Select your data. Pick a year and an antigen or infection type. Apply filters to refresh the results.
  3. Read the units. Distinguish reported cases, target-group coverage and rates relative to total population.
  4. Explore further. Compare two years in vaccination changes, or compare infection rates with the global benchmark.

Read the figures with care

Data notes & plain-language definitions

Antigen
A label for the vaccine or dose category in this dataset. The selector shows each code alongside its name.
Vaccination coverage
The reported percentage of a target group reached. When reported coverage is missing, a calculated value may use valid doses divided by the target count; the results identify this source. It is different from doses divided by the total country population.
Herd immunity
Community-level protection is not directly measured by this database. The regional page shows coverage and counts meeting a comparison threshold; these are not a determination of community immunity.
Population-based vaccination rate
Administered doses ÷ total population × 100, for one antigen and one year. This is an administered-dose rate, not the percentage of unique people vaccinated. Level 3A compares this rate between two years.
Percentage points
The subtraction of two percentage rates. This differs from a relative percentage change.
Infection rate per 100,000
Reported cases ÷ population for the same country and year × 100,000. A common denominator makes countries of different sizes easier to compare.
Global benchmark
Total reported cases ÷ total population of countries with valid matching records × 100,000. This is population-weighted, not a simple average of country rates. “Global” describes the available dataset, not complete worldwide surveillance.
Economic status & regions
Labels come directly from the supplied database. Economic status is a single classification per country, not a historical classification for each year. Country names may also represent territories.
Known data limitations

The source contains {{ quality.missing_doses|num }} nonnumeric dose values and {{ quality.missing_coverage|num }} nonnumeric coverage values. These are treated as missing source values. Missing coverage may have a calculated fallback when paired doses and targets are valid. {{ quality.invalid_coverage|num }} coverage values lie outside 0–100%. Nonnegative values above 100% are retained and flagged; negative values are not used as reported coverage. No value is silently capped.

{{ quality.orphan_vaccinations|num }} vaccination records have no matching country metadata. {{ quality.unknown_economies|num }} countries have no valid economic classification. Geographical comparisons require matching countries; economy comparisons also require a valid classification.

Numeric zero values are retained as reported. A reported zero does not establish an absence of disease. Coverage fallbacks are calculated and labelled; missing infection cases and population are not imputed. Rates require a positive numeric population for the same year. Doses and targets can have reporting anomalies, and target figures include extreme outliers; they should not be treated as validated individual-person counts.

Source: immunisation-2.db supplied with the course assignment. The database contains no field-level source citations or reporting-method notes. This website does not add causal conclusions or medical advice.
